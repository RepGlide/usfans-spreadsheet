Category Lens Guide

Why category decides the questions, with concise observation notes for all twelve categories.

Why category comes first

The right question for a sneaker is not the right question for a perfume. Identify the product type before you ask anything, so each row is checked against questions that actually fit it.

Concise notes for each category

1. Bags

Look at the silhouette, strap, and hardware. Note the label angle and any size or material statement you can see.

2. Women’s Clothing

Check the cut, the size label, and any page dimensions. Fit needs your own measurements, not just a tag.

3. Men’s Clothing

Check the shape and the size label, and note any stated dimensions. Confirm the size against the listing.

4. Sneakers

Look at the silhouette, the toebox, the side profile, and the insole or size label where visible.

5. Designer Shoes

Check the shape, the sole, and the label angle. Note what the photos do and do not show about the build.

6. Designer Women’s Shoes

Check the heel, the shape, and the label angle. Confirm the size and the version before relying on the row.

7. Perfume

Note the bottle and label. Scent cannot be seen, so what a photo shows is limited to the packaging.

8. Watches

Look at the face, the case, and the back. Note the visible markings without reading them as proof.

9. Jerseys

Check the shape, the numbering, and the label angle. Confirm the size against the listing.

10. Accessories

Note the item type and its visible detail. Confirm the option and any stated material on the page.

11. Underwear & Swimwear

Check the cut and the size label. Fit needs your own measurements; a label alone is not a fit.

12. Other Finds

Identify the item first, then pick the closest category lens. If none fits, keep the questions generic.
